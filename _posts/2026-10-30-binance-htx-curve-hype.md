{% capture t %}On Friday, January 26, 2018, the Tokyo-based crypto exchange Coincheck found that hackers had sent about 523 million NEM coins out of its systems [17][18]. The company said the coins were worth about 58 billion yen, some $534 million at the time [17]. The unauthorized transfers had started at 02:57 local time, but the breach was only found at 11:25, nearly eight and a half hours later [17]. The coins sat in a hot wallet, which is connected to the internet, instead of an offline cold wallet [17][18]. Coincheck said it had not used cold storage because of technical difficulties and a shortage of staff able to handle it [18]. It froze deposits and withdrawals of every cryptocurrency except bitcoin while it checked what had happened [17][18]. Two days later, it said it would repay about 46.3 billion yen from its own cash to the roughly 260,000 customers who held the stolen NEM [18].
