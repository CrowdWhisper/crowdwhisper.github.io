**Attribution.** Blockchain analytics firm TRM Labs attributes the Binance theft to TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking unit, and says it makes this judgment with high confidence <a class="cite" href="#src-1">[1]</a>. CoinDesk reports that investigator ZachXBT also called the attacker "likely Lazarus" and that security firm Blockaid called the tactics consistent with North Korea-linked operations <a class="cite" href="#src-2">[2]</a>. Binance itself has not named an attacker.

**Size.** TRM counts about $6.36 billion moved out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains, most of it in about 20 minutes from 20:05 UTC on 29 October <a class="cite" href="#src-1">[1]</a>. Binance's own estimate is about $6.3 billion <a class="cite" href="#src-3">[3]</a><a class="cite" href="#src-4">[4]</a>.

**Frozen and still out.** TRM says Tether, Circle, Ripple and World Liberty Financial froze about $2.67 billion in stablecoins, and Arbitrum's Security Council froze further ETH and ARB, about $2.7 billion in total; the attacker still holds more than 420,000 ETH and 18,270 BTC, about $2.4 billion beyond any issuer's reach <a class="cite" href="#src-1">[1]</a>.

**Withdrawals.** Binance co-CEO Yi He said on X at 04:17 UTC that deposits operate normally and that the exchange expects to begin resuming withdrawals within the next 48 hours while it improves the withdrawal service and address controls <a class="cite" href="#src-5">[5]</a><a class="cite" href="#src-6">[6]</a>. Binance's official account repeated this at 04:36 UTC: deposits are unaffected and withdrawals stay paused during a security revamp, with resumption expected within 48 hours <a class="cite" href="#src-7">[7]</a>. No exact time or order of networks was given. Binance says user funds are safe and that it will cover all losses <a class="cite" href="#src-3">[3]</a><a class="cite" href="#src-4">[4]</a>.
