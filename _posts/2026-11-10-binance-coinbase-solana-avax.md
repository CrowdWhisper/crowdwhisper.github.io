{% capture t %}**Binance hack** launderers created at least six throwaway meme tokens on NEAR between 5 and 7 November and used them to move stolen funds through decentralized-exchange liquidity pools, blockchain analytics firm Elliptic said on 9 November [1][2]. About 117,000 NEAR, roughly $430,000, passed through the tokens, while about 11.8 million NEAR from the theft has not moved yet, according to Elliptic [1]. Elliptic, which attributes the October attack to North Korea, says the trail can still be followed on-chain and the method slows investigators down rather than hiding the funds [1].{% endcapture %}

{% capture t %}**Coinbase** said on 9 November that the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency gave final approval to charter Coinbase National Trust Company [3][5]. The regulator's records show the charter took effect on 6 November; the trust bank holds digital assets in custody, mainly for institutions, and cannot take deposits or make loans [4]. Coinbase plans to move the custody business of its New York trust company into the new federal bank over a three-year window [4].{% endcapture %}

{% capture t %}**Solana** developers proposed retiring durable nonces, a feature that keeps a signed transaction valid instead of letting it expire after about 30 seconds [6][8]. The draft, SIMD-0723, was opened on 5 November and says the change will not be activated on mainnet before 5 May 2027; CoinDesk reports it still needs sign-off from engineers at Anza and Firedancer [6][7]. Attackers relied on durable nonces in the April attack that drained at least $270 million from Drift, CoinDesk notes, although the draft itself argues from years of validator bugs [7].{% endcapture %}
