**Outflow.** More than $2 billion in crypto left Binance-labelled hot wallets on Ethereum from 20:24 UTC on 29 October, according to Etherscan data reported by CoinDesk, Decrypt, BeInCrypto and FinanceFeeds [1][2][3][5]. Security firm Blockaid puts the amount moved at nearly $3.3 billion, CoinDesk reports [1]. Hot wallets are the internet-connected wallets an exchange uses to pay out customer withdrawals. One on-chain tally cited by Decrypt puts the total at about $5.57 billion across Ethereum, Bitcoin and Tron; other trackers report lower figures, so the final amount is not yet clear [2][3].

**Method.** Etherscan data shows the transfers were triggered by an address funded through the Tornado Cash mixer, minutes after an EIP-7702 delegation was set on the main hot wallet [2][3]. EIP-7702 is an Ethereum feature that lets an ordinary wallet run smart-contract code; setting it requires a valid signature, and how that signature was obtained is unknown [2][3].

**Binance.** Binance said on X that it is aware of a potential security issue, is investigating, and has not confirmed any impact to user funds [1][2]. Former CEO Changpeng Zhao wrote that it looks like a hack and that he expects user funds to be fully covered [1][3].

**Freeze.** Tether froze about $971 million to $975 million in USDT and Circle froze about $823 million in USDC at the main receiving address, about $1.8 billion in total, Decrypt and CoinDesk report; Circle's CEO confirmed the USDC freeze [1][2].

**Markets.** BNB, Binance's own token, was down about 7% over 24 hours and Bitcoin traded near $79,000 at 21:46 UTC, CoinGecko data shows [4].
